Reading the news is not about knowing more events. It is about noticing what the world is beginning to need. Somewhere inside that movement, you have to find the point where what you want to do meets what you are capable of doing well. And in that one area, you need to go deep enough to understand it like someone with a doctorate.

AI is a tool for reaching that depth faster. You give it the relevant papers, research and source material, then use it to extract what you actually need to learn. Instead of spending all your time reading everything yourself, you spend more time deciding what is worth understanding.

Once that knowledge passes a certain threshold, what once felt vague begins to take shape.

What should I do?  
Which problem should I solve?  
How could that solution become a business?

When the goal and business model become clear, you can communicate them clearly to AI. You review the output, point out what is missing, and ask it to try again. Through that repetition, the work improves. Organizing that entire process into a coherent system is what building a business really is.

But there must always be a person at the end of it. Knowing more is not valuable by itself. The value comes from turning that knowledge into products and services that help other people. The world is already overflowing with information. What people need is not more of it. They need help seeing what matters inside the noise.

Connecting what is scattered.  
Simplifying what is complicated.  
Making something easier to understand, easier to use and more useful in real life.

Perhaps creating real value is not always about adding something new to the world.  
Perhaps it is about creating order from the chaos that is already here.
