Why do we want to be successful?

So we can live on our own terms without worrying about what people think?  
So nobody can look down on us?  
So our family and friends can be proud of us?

A lot of the time, it seems we’re more focused on how other people see us than on what we actually want. Of course, we share our lives with others. We can’t help caring what they think. The problem is, there’s no end to how much recognition we can want.

No matter how well we do, there’s always someone above us, someone ahead. And then we fix our eyes on them and start chasing again.

Fear.

How do I come across? Where do I stand? Am I falling behind?

The more that fear grows, the more we want reassurance from other people. We feel we need to earn more money. Maybe then we’ll finally feel secure.

So what about love?

I don’t just mean love between partners. I mean being able to care about someone without constantly checking whether they care just as much about us. Seeing someone else do well without feeling jealous. Feeling that we don’t always have to prove something. Believing we’re not alone in this world.

Put like that, fear sounds like the villain. But we need it too. It protects us when we’re in danger. And loving someone doesn’t make fear disappear. Once someone matters to us, we can become afraid of losing them.

The mistakes I’ve made. The relationships that went wrong. The regrets I still carry.

I can’t say fear caused all of them. But I think it’s worth asking how often I stepped back because I was afraid of being rejected, of losing something, of trying and failing.

Fear can make us hide our feelings even when we like someone. Question their kindness. Want to get closer, then pull away because we don’t want to get hurt.

Fear is hard to give up all at once, a bit like smoking. But just as we might cut down on the number of cigarettes we smoke each day, perhaps we can loosen our fear’s hold a little at a time. I like to think love could find its way into the space that leaves behind.

Maybe love is something we wait for, rather than something we chase.  
Until we’re ready to let it in.
